## MAIN POST (~1,180 chars)

A conference is two things at once: the meeting in the rooms, and the portrait the programme paints of the community putting it on.

For ASLO-SIL 2026 in Montreal this week, the second portrait is striking. I read all 1,455 scheduled presentations across 308 session items before flying. Six things the schedule itself says, in numbers, verbs, and invitations:

→ Most-repeated session-title phrases: "multiple stressors" ×9, "bridging the gap" ×8, "towards a convergence of current knowledge and application" ×6.

→ Three career-tribute sessions (Pace, Cotner, Elser) in the same programme as six dedicated early-career sessions (Amplifying Voices, ECR alliance, "How To," Raelyn Cole Fellowship retrospective).

→ Freshwater science is a major center of gravity: lakes 28%, microbial 18%, biogeochem 17%. About 93% of talks carry ≥1 dominant tag.

→ Indigenous knowledge, equity, and community-led science across 14 session items. EP013 "Two-Eyed Seeing" is a science session.

→ Method tags: AI/ML 2.4%, eDNA/-omics 3.2%, mass spec 2%, long-term monitoring 9% (the most-tagged single method).

→ A map for future invitations: US+Canada = 54% of presenters. The largest freshwater-stock countries (Brazil, Russia, Colombia, Indonesia, Peru, India, Myanmar — together ≈42% of global freshwater) are under-represented or absent. Future travel pathways, hybrid options, and regional partnerships have real room to grow.

(Full data, reproducible analysis: see comments.)

Methodology: scraped the full session gallery from the ASLO-SIL public site (1,455 scheduled presentations across 308 session items, audited 11 May 2026). Tagged presentations by methodology and disciplinary frame using keyword classifiers (multi-label; approximate). Country detection is keyword-based on affiliation strings + email-domain TLDs — about 78% coverage; small countries undercounted.
