# ASLO-SIL 2026 — executive summary *(v3, audited + reframed)*

*A one-page version of the longer member-narrative piece. ~470 words. For talks, intros, or a busy editor. Every assertion verified against the underlying data.*

---

**1,455 scheduled presentations. 308 session items. About 1,400 primary presenters. About 740 institutions. Five days in Montreal.**

I read the full ASLO-SIL 2026 programme before flying. Six things the schedule itself says:

**One — the most-repeated phrases are about working across.** "Multiple stressor(s)" appears nine times in session titles. "Bridging the gap" appears eight. The exact frame *"Towards a Convergence of Current Knowledge and Application"* and variants appear six.

**Two — three legacies, six early-career sessions, in one programme.** Career-tribute sessions for Mike Pace (EP001), Jim Cotner (EP004), and Jim Elser (EP012) sit in the same five-day programme as AV001 "Amplifying Voices," EC02 (ECR resilience), EC03 (cross-organisational ECR alliance), EP010A/B, EP011 (first-timer "How To"), and EP006 (Raelyn Cole Fellowship retrospective).

**Three — freshwater science is a major center of gravity.** A keyword classifier (multi-label) finds lakes and limnology in about 28% of talks, microbial ecology in about 18%, biogeochemistry in about 17%, climate change as a forcing in about 10%, marine in about 9%, rivers in about 9%, biodiversity in about 7%, estuarine and coastal in about 6%, food webs in about 5%. About 93% of talks carry at least one of these tags. Freshwater systems together (lakes + rivers + wetlands) are about 39%; saltwater (marine + estuarine) about 15%.

**Four — the problem-cluster sessions name specific integrative questions.** SS002 (gas fluxes across the aquatic continuum), SS050 (DOM persistence), nine sessions on multiple stressors, SS011/SS013/SS015/SS019 (cryosphere and drought), SS010 (nutrient accounting), SS039 (wildfires and aquatic systems), SS029 and SS048 (HAB processes and cyanobacteria adaptations), SS058 (microbial-DOM coupling), SS030 (eco-evolutionary feedbacks), SS082 and SS070 (scale integration).

**Five — modest, mapped method signatures.** AI/ML tags in about 2.4% of talks. eDNA and -omics tags in about 3.2%. Mass spectrometry in about 33 talks (about 2%), 14 of those using ultra-high-resolution MS (FT-ICR, Orbitrap), 7 naming FT-ICR specifically. Long-term monitoring tags in about 9%, the most-tagged single method.

**Six — geography points to where the next invitations could be strongest.** Indigenous-knowledge, equity, community-led, and citizen-science framings appear across 14 session items (about 58 talks). EP013 "Two-Eyed Seeing" is a programmed science session; WS05 and WS08 are dedicated workshops. The US (about 29%) and Canada (about 25%) together account for about 54% of presenters. Japan comes closest to parity between its program share (1.4%) and its global freshwater share (1.0%). Several countries with much larger freshwater stocks are under-represented relative to that stake: Brazil (about 13% of global freshwater, about 1.3% of the programme), Russia (about 10%), Colombia (5%), Indonesia (5%), Peru (4%), India (3%), Myanmar (2%) — together about 42% of the world's renewable freshwater. Read generously, that is a map for future collaboration: travel pathways, hybrid options, regional mentorship, and more routes for freshwater-rich countries to shape the agenda.
