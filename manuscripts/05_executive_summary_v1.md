# ASLO-SIL 2026 — executive summary

*A one-page version of the longer member-narrative piece. ~400 words. For tweets, talks, intros, or a busy editor.*

---

**1,455 presentations. 308 sessions. About 1,400 presenters from 740 institutions. Five days in Montreal.**

I read the full ASLO-SIL 2026 program before flying. Six things stand out.

**One — the verbs have changed.** The dominant words in 2026 session titles are *bridge, integrate, converge, scale*, not *measure, describe, characterise*. "Multiple stressors" appears nine times as a session bigram, "bridging the gap" eight, "towards convergence" six. The field has collected enough evidence; it is now asking what that evidence can bear.

**Two — three legacies, scaffolded by a generation.** Mike Pace, Jim Cotner, and Jim Elser are honored simultaneously in tribute sessions. The honors sit deliberately beside an early-career scaffold: AV001 Amplifying Voices, EC02 ECR resilience, EC03 cross-organisational ECR alliance, EP011 first-timer "How To," EP006 Raelyn Cole Fellowship retrospective. The meeting is handing off in public.

**Three — it is a freshwater meeting.** Lakes and limnology carry about 28% of the program, rivers and wetlands another 11%. Marine oceanography is the minority at 9%. The SIL half of the joint name is doing real work; Montreal venue helped. Microbial ecology (~18%) and biogeochemistry (~17%) are the connective tissue.

**Four — the field is tackling integrative problems.** The session descriptions name nine problem clusters: (1) the carbon balance of inland waters, (2) interaction of multiple stressors, (3) climate-driven change including browning, drying, ice loss, (4) eutrophication under altered hydrology, (5) disturbance and recovery — fire, logging, urbanization, (6) harmful algal blooms and their mitigation, (7) microbial-DOM coupling, (8) eco-evolutionary feedbacks, (9) scale integration from molecular detail to ecosystem flux.

**Five — modest, pointed bets on the future.** AI/ML in 2.4% of talks. eDNA/-omics in 3.2%. Mass spectrometry in 2%, with 14 talks using ultra-high-resolution MS (FT-ICR, Orbitrap). Long-term monitoring holds at 9% — the field's methodological soul. None of these is a wave; all are deliberate uptakes.

**Six — inclusivity is in the architecture, not the brochure.** Indigenous knowledge, equity, and community-led science appear in 58 talks across 14 session items. EP013 "Two-Eyed Seeing" is programmed as a science session. About 56% of session organisers and ~53% of presenters are female-inferred — gender parity reached at both levels. The geographic gap remains: the US and Canada together account for about 54% of presenters; Brazil is the only top-20 country whose meeting participation roughly matches its global freshwater share.

**The portrait:** strong identity, confident craft, room to grow. A field in synthesis mode, deciding out loud who helps shape what it learns next.
